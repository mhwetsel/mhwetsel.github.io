abstract: |
  Why are some Eurosceptic parties more successful than others? I posed this question over ten years ago, when the fallout from the Eurozone debt crisis was increasing polarization between core and peripheral member
  states, and the post-Arab Spring migrant crisis was challenging many commonly held beliefs that underlie the European project. Specifically, the value of Europe as an “open” society that allows for the free movement of
  people and trade, which is consistent with and strengthens the Union’s commitment to liberal democracy, became the subject of a contentious debate within and amongst the various member states. Scholars sought to
  establish a causal link between exogenous shocks and the marked increase in public support for populist parties that championed Eurosceptic policies, such as an end to fiscal austerity and the securitization of
  national borders. Whether it was through carefully calculated strategy or a confluence of fortuitous circumstances, Eurosceptic parties were able to translate popular dissatisfaction with mainstream political
  institutions into significant electoral gains.

  In the intervening years, a succession of crises has continued to erode the public’s faith in mainstream political parties and the institutional framework of the European Union: Brexit, Covid, and the War in Ukraine.
  My paper argues that existing theoretical frameworks cannot account for variation in the levels of Euroscepticism observed across time and space because they are overly parsimonious, with respect to sui generis contexts,
  i.e., the institutional path dependencies of member states. Using a mixed methods research design, I combine an interdisciplinary literature review with a multivariate, OLS regression analysis of survey panel data to
  evaluate the veracity of my argument. Preliminary findings indicate that changes to perceived levels of insecurity and social cohesion, within the broader context of rising global uncertainty, can provide an encompassing
  explanation for varying levels of Euroscepticism.
